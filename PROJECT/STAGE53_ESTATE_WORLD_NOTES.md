# Private island terrain and buildings

The privately owned island sits east of the city. Its terrain uses the same authored shoreline and height functions for walking, driving, ferry approaches, and runway elevation. An illuminated pier reaches the helipad and west-coast road. The airfield provides a 640 m east–west runway at elevation 4.2 m, a marked apron, and routes from the airport to the rental district. The ferry has a vehicle unload point on the mainland-facing island road. The airport holds five planned aircraft parking spaces. Garage and dock locations use separate sites for five cars and five boats.

The existing road and transit modules retain their destination identifiers. The new transport stop is `estate`. A walkable path leads from the dock to the airfield road and village. The island's office is at world coordinates (1750, 480). The three paid duplex plots contain paired units a1/a2, b1/b2, and c1/c2. Housing purchases build one duplex at a time. Each apartment has a separate threshold, walkable interior, room light, and camera bounds. The sliding doors open on approach or with **E**. Paid furniture places beds, seating, and tables along the walls with a clear route from each door. Approved tenants leave and return through their assigned door. They rotate between a local stroll, visiting the garden after it opens, and meeting near their duplex. They greet a nearby visitor with a clear sightline, pause and face them while speaking, and step away from an approaching vehicle before resuming their route. The island does not provide any available rental unit before the first housing purchase.

Geometry follows the saved development levels. Restoring earlier management state removes superseded paid meshes, collision volumes, room state, and tenants while preserving the island's public approach surfaces.

`node QA/stage53_estate_world_acceptance.cjs` runs the packaged game code with a simulated display. It exercises the actual player movement and collision, the runway and harbor, paid apartment entrances, door activation, tenancy approval, greeting and conversation holds, vehicle avoidance, daily routines, actor submission, and an earlier-state restore. The renderer's displayed pixels and frame rate require real game hardware.
